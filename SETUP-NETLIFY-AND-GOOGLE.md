# BeyondClass: set up after buying your domain

Prepared for Ekemini Akpan • Revised 15 October 2026

**Save this guide and the website ZIP now. Begin the setup below after you have bought your domain.**

Netlify will remain your hosting provider. Your own domain will become the public address. Keep the existing **beyondclassmath** Netlify project throughout this process.

This workflow sets up Google for your final domain directly. You can skip Google verification and sitemap submission for the temporary `beyondclassmath.netlify.app` address.

## For now

Save **BeyondClass-Netlify-Source.zip** and this guide. Continue reviewing the local website if you want. Leave the domain, Google and production inbox setup until you have your domain.

The new hosted inbox and page counts become available after the deployment below. The prepared local updates have not yet been published to your Netlify website.

## 1. Connect your purchased domain

1. Sign in at https://app.netlify.com/ and open **beyondclassmath**.
2. Open **Domain management → Production domains → Add a domain → Add a domain you already own**. Enter the domain you bought.
3. Follow Netlify's DNS instructions. The records depend on whether you use Netlify DNS or your domain company's DNS service.
4. Select your preferred address as the **primary domain**. Decide whether it includes `www` and use that same address below.
5. Wait for the DNS connection and HTTPS certificate to become active. Check that your chosen address opens securely before the main deployment.

You can connect the domain to the existing published site first. The updated website files will be published in step 4.

**Write down your final HTTPS address.** `https://yourdomain.com` below is a placeholder; replace it with your actual primary address.

## 2. Verify the final domain with Google using DNS

1. Open https://search.google.com/search-console/ and sign in with your business's Google account.
2. Choose **Add property → Domain**. Enter your bare domain, such as `yourdomain.com`, without `https://`, `www` or a path.
3. Copy Google's DNS TXT verification record.
4. Add it at the service managing your active DNS: Netlify if you selected Netlify DNS, otherwise your DNS provider. Add a new record without replacing existing TXT records.
5. Return to Google and click **Verify** after the record becomes visible. Keep that TXT record afterward because Google checks it again.

This method covers the domain's protocol and subdomain variations. It requires no Google HTML verification file, website meta tag or extra website deployment for verification. Leave `GOOGLE_SITE_VERIFICATION` unset when using this method.

## 3. Configure the launch settings before publishing

In Netlify, open **Project configuration → Environment variables**. Add these values for the **production** context:

| Setting | Your value | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SITE_URL` | Your exact primary HTTPS address, including `www` if you chose it | Builds |
| `INBOX_PASSWORD` | A unique password with at least 16 characters, preferably generated by a password manager | Functions |

If separate scopes are unavailable, choose **All scopes**. Keep the password in your password manager.

The build uses `SITE_URL` to generate your final sitemap, robots file, canonical links and sharing metadata. It also prepares redirects from `beyondclassmath.netlify.app` to your domain. You do not need to rewrite those files manually.

If your Stripe Payment Links are ready, add the public `https://buy.stripe.com/...` links to their matching plans in `assets/checkout-config.js` before publishing. Otherwise, checkout can be connected later. Keep secret keys out of website files.

The Payment page already includes the Cleva USD and OPay NGN receiving details you supplied. After publishing, check both currency selections and send a test transfer notice to confirm it appears in your private inbox. A notice does not move money or confirm a payment: check the receiving account yourself before telling a parent their payment has arrived.

## 4. Publish the updated website

1. Extract the latest **BeyondClass-Netlify-Source.zip** into a folder on your PC.
2. Ensure Node.js 22.13 or newer is installed. It is already available on the PC used to edit the website; on another PC, get the current LTS release from https://nodejs.org/.
3. In the extracted folder, double-click **Deploy-BeyondClass.cmd**.
4. Sign in through Netlify if asked. Check that the helper links to the existing **beyondclassmath** project.
5. After completing steps 1–3 of this guide, type **PUBLISH** when asked. The helper installs the dependency, builds the pages and inbox function, and deploys them. Keep the window open until Netlify confirms success.

The project's name stays **beyondclassmath** after adding a custom domain. Your primary-domain and `SITE_URL` settings determine the public address.

Use the helper or a connected repository. A plain Netlify Drop upload of the source ZIP does not build the inbox function. For a connected repository, `netlify.toml` supplies the build command **npm run build**, publish directory **dist** and functions directory **netlify/functions**. Keep credentials and private local folders out of the repository.

## 5. Check everything on your final domain

1. Browse the home page and other pages on a computer and phone.
2. Submit a free-materials request using your own email address.
3. Open `/inbox.html` on your domain, sign in with your new password and confirm the request appears. Test a trial request too.
4. Check **Website activity** in the inbox for today's views, the daily chart and counts by page.
5. Open `/sitemap.xml` and `/robots.txt` on your domain. Confirm their addresses use the final domain.
6. Check that the old `https://beyondclassmath.netlify.app/` address redirects to your domain.

Netlify stores new hosted requests privately. Local requests on your PC are separate and are not included in the public deployment package. Review the inbox and follow up with parents; saving a request does not automatically email materials or confirm a lesson.

## 6. Submit the final sitemap to Google

1. Open your verified **Domain property** in Google Search Console.
2. Go to **Sitemaps**, enter the full final sitemap URL—for example `https://yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml`—and click **Submit**. Use your actual primary domain.
3. Use **URL inspection** for the home page and key pages such as Pricing and Contact. Choose **Request indexing** when offered.
4. Check Search Console later for indexing status and reported issues.

Submit the sitemap's **live URL**, not the ZIP or a local file. The build publishes `robots.txt` automatically; it needs no separate submission. Google decides when to crawl and index pages, and submission does not guarantee a ranking.

## What you can avoid repeating

You will set up the final domain's Google property directly. This workflow has no earlier temporary-address verification, temporary sitemap submission or later Google domain move.

Keep the domain connected, retain Google's DNS TXT record and use the same Netlify project. Future content changes can require a new deployment, but they do not require repeating the domain setup and Google verification while those settings remain valid.

## Inbox and page counts after launch

To change your hosted inbox password later, update `INBOX_PASSWORD` in Netlify and redeploy. Previous sessions will stop working after that deployment.

Page counts show approximate public page loads, including repeat visits, rather than unique people. Dates use UTC. The inbox is excluded. Records contain only page names, dates and totals. Count requests omit login cookies, referrers and query strings and respect Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. Counts start after deployment; earlier Netlify traffic is not imported.

The dashboard uses your existing Netlify Functions and storage allowances. No separate analytics account or paid analytics add-on is required.
